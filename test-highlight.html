The Diversity of Ocean Ecosystems

The ocean covers more than 70% of Earth's surface, yet we understand less about it than we do about the moon. Below the waves exists a vast and complex world of ecosystems, each adapted to unique environmental conditions. From the sunlit tropical coral reefs to the crushing depths of the abyssal zone, marine life demonstrates extraordinary diversity and resilience.

Coral reefs, often called the rainforests of the sea, support more than a quarter of all known marine species despite covering less than 0.1% of the ocean floor. These vibrant ecosystems rely on a delicate balance between coral polyps and symbiotic algae called zooxanthellae. When water temperatures rise due to climate change, this partnership breaks down, leading to coral bleaching and ecosystem collapse.

The ocean's twilight zone, or mesopelagic zone, exists between 200 and 1000 meters below the surface. Here, organisms have developed bioluminescence as a primary means of communication and hunting. The density of life in this zone is estimated to be 10 times greater than previously thought, suggesting vast untapped resources and unknown species waiting to be discovered.
